# JOSS Software Review Presentations and Project Transfer

Today you will share what you learned from reviewing a published JOSS project and identify how that evidence will improve your own final project. The goal is to collect practical examples of research software practices that make projects easier to use, evaluate, and continue.

## Agenda (80 minutes)

1. 10 min - Finalize presentation notes and review card
2. 55 min - Student presentations and brief questions
3. 10 min - Identify recurring practices worth adopting
4. 5 min - Commit to one release-candidate improvement

### Learning Goals

By the end of this class, you should be able to:

- communicate evidence-based observations about an unfamiliar research software project; and
- translate an observed software practice into a concrete improvement for your own release candidate.

## Student Presentations

Each student will have about two minutes to present, followed by a brief question or transition. Use your [JOSS Software Review Card](../Guide/22-JOSS-Software-Review-Card.md) to cover:

1. The JOSS project and the problem it solves.
2. One observed strength, with specific evidence from the paper or repository.
3. One improvement opportunity or limitation.
4. One practice you will apply, adapt, or intentionally avoid in your own project.

Focus on what you observed rather than making claims about code you did not examine. A failed installation, a confusing workflow, or a missing explanation can be valuable evidence when described professionally and specifically.

## Whole-Class Synthesis

As classmates present, listen for practices that make research software more useful to future collaborators:

- clear README structure and quickstart paths;
- examples, tutorials, and expected outputs;
- environment and dependency guidance;
- tests or validation checks;
- release, citation, and contribution information;
- limitations and appropriate-use statements; and
- clear explanation of the software's research contribution.

Record at least one idea from another student's presentation that may help your own project.

## Apply the Review to Your Project

Before leaving class, add one feasible improvement to your release-candidate fix plan. Choose an action that can be completed or substantially advanced before the release-candidate milestone, such as clarifying a setup step, adding an expected output, documenting a limitation, or improving a test or example.
