# Final Presentations - Day 1

The final presentations are a chance to share how your course project advances or supports your research. Present the project as a research software artifact: show what it does, why it matters in your research context, and how another researcher could understand the work.

## Agenda (80 minutes)

1. 5 min - Review presentation order, timing, and peer-evaluation assignments
2. 65 min - Final project presentations, following the order shown on the class slide
3. 10 min - Peer-evaluation check-in, feedback reminders, and transition to Day 2

## Learning Goals

By the end of this class, you should be able to:

- explain how your project, its evidence, and its software workflow connect to your research; and
- give concise, constructive feedback on a peer's project as a future research collaborator.

## Presentation Expectations

Plan for approximately **10 minutes** for your presentation. A small amount of flexibility is expected so that presenters can demonstrate a meaningful part of their project and answer essential questions. Keep the presentation focused on:

- the research question, problem, or need the project addresses;
- how the project fits into or supports your research;
- the main workflow, result, or software contribution;
- a short demonstration of the working project, result, or documented workflow; and
- one limitation, next step, or improvement you would make with more time.

Show only work that is represented accurately in your final repository and documentation. If your project uses restricted data or services, demonstrate a shareable synthetic, example, or mock workflow instead.

## Presentation Technology

The classroom computer will be set up with Zoom, using the same presentation workflow used throughout the semester. Choose the option that best supports your presentation and demonstration:

1. Open your slides on the classroom computer and share that screen.
2. Connect to the classroom Zoom meeting from your own laptop and share your screen.

If you use your own laptop, confirm before your presentation that you can join the Zoom meeting, turn off both your microphone and speakers to prevent audio feedback, and share your screen. Using your own laptop is encouraged when it will make a project demonstration smoother.

As an alternative, send the instructor a presentation URL before your scheduled presentation. The instructor can add the link to the presentation-order slide and open it from the classroom computer.

## Presentation Order and Peer Evaluation

The class slide will list presenters in order. It will also identify the three students assigned to review each presentation. Follow that slide for both presentation order and reviewer assignments.

If you are assigned as a reviewer, complete one online peer evaluation for each of your three assigned presentations. Your feedback should be specific, evidence-based, and useful to the presenter. Note a strength, a question or observation about the research/software connection, and one actionable suggestion related to Safe, Portable, Reproducible, Robust, or Literate practice.
