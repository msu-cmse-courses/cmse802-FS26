# CMSE 802

CMSE 802 is a graduate course in research software engineering for modeling and simulation. The course centers on building reliable, transparent, and reproducible computational workflows for scientific research.

Students work across the full cycle of modeling practice: formulating a problem, testing assumptions, generating and interpreting data, fitting models, and evaluating whether the results are credible in context. The course emphasizes practical software habits alongside analytical thinking, with a focus on making research methods easier to reproduce, verify, and communicate.

## Course Themes

- Safe, portable, reproducible, robust, and literate scientific workflows
- Modeling as an iterative process rather than a one-time calculation
- Data generation, estimation, and model criticism as core scientific practices
- Version control, testing, documentation, and clear project structure
- Responsible use of computational tools in research settings

## Course Structure

The semester is organized around a connected sequence of modeling and software activities. Students move from analytical reasoning and symbolic models to simulated or noisy observations, then to parameter estimation, comparison, and evaluation of models in realistic settings.

The course is designed to help students build confidence in both:

- the scientific interpretation of models, and
- the software engineering practices needed to support research-quality work.

## Goals

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- formulate and analyze model-based questions in a computational context,
- build reproducible workflows for data generation and model fitting,
- evaluate the assumptions and limitations of a model,
- communicate results clearly in a way that others can reproduce and review,
- use good software engineering practices in research projects.
